How Animals Sleep

12 pages · photo · narrator: Documentary — A documentary narrator would tell this aloud, describing wild animals and their sleeping habits from the outside.

Introduction Almost all animals need to sleep to stay healthy. But sleeping can be dangerous. Other animals may hunt sleeping animals. Animals safe. Some you!

Sleeping Upside Down Almost all bats sleep upside down. They hang by their toes from a branch or from the ceiling of a cave. Up high, bats are safe from most other animals. If they are in danger, they just fly away.

Sleeping with Half the Brain A dolphin sleeps with just half its brain at a time. It must stay awake to breathe and to watch for danger. Dolphin mothers and babies can go without sleep for as long as two months to stay safe.
Picture: A dolphin rests in the sea, sleeping with just half its brain at a time. It stays awake enough to breathe and watch for danger. A dolphin mother and her baby swim close together, able to go without sleep for up to two months.

Sleeping Standing Up Horses often sleep standing up. Their legs lock in place so they don’t fall over. If a horse is lying down, it needs a long time to get up. A horse can run from danger more quickly if it is already standing.

Picture: A flamingo sleeps while standing on one leg. The standing leg locks straight, the other leg tucks up close to the body, and its head rests on its back. Many other birds stand nearby, helping the flamingo stay safe.

Sleeping While Flying Some seabirds can sleep while they fly. These large birds spend most of their lives over the ocean. They are in danger on the water, but they are safe in the air. They glide for many hours without flapping their wings. Sometimes they sleep while they glide.
Picture: A large seabird glides over the ocean for many hours without flapping its wings. It is safe in the air, though in danger on the water. Sometimes the seabird sleeps while it glides above the sea.

1
Introduction Animals have many ways to defend themselves and stay safe. Some have sharp claws, teeth, or horns. Others change color or hide in a shell. Some animals simply run away from danger. Plants can’t bite, fight, or run away. However, they have many other ways to protect themselves.

Picture: Rose stems with prickles growing out of the soft stem, alongside small shrubs and flowering plants. Another plant shows thorns on a tree or large shrub, making clear that prickles and thorns grow in different places on different kinds of plants.

Chichén Itzá

A Building in the Jungle Long ago, ancestors of the Maya people built a city in the jungle. The city is named Chichén Itzá (chee-CHEN eet-SAH). The name means “at the mouth of the well of Itzá.” This city is an important place in the history of the Maya people.

Picture: A wide view of the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico: thick green jungle and forest stretch across the land, with patches of farmland and a few large cities in the distance. Chichén Itzá's stone buildings stand among the trees.

An Old City Chichén Itzá was built many hundreds of years ago by the Maya people. They made stone buildings that still stand today. The building at the center of the city is a tall stepped pyramid.

A Special Spot The Maya had an important reason to build a city where they did. This part of Mexico has no rivers or lakes. Instead, fresh water comes up from underground wells called cenotes (say-NOH-tayz). Chichén Itzá has two cenotes.
Picture: A special spot in the Mexican landscape with no rivers or lakes in sight. Instead, round openings in the ground lead down to underground wells called cenotes, where clear fresh water pools. Two of these cenotes lie near Chichén Itzá.

Picture: Maya people lower containers into one cenote to draw fresh water for drinking. At the other cenote, people set out gifts for Chac, the rain god, hoping he will send rain to help their corn and vegetables grow in nearby fields.
9
There are many important gods in Maya culture. Many buildings at Chichén Itzá are temples. The temples are places where people can give gifts to the gods. The stepped pyramid has a temple at the top for a god that looks like a snake with wings. This is the Maya god of creation.
Picture: Maya temples stand throughout Chichén Itzá, places where people give gifts to their gods. At the top of the stepped pyramid, a temple honors the Maya god of creation, shown as a snake with wings carved in stone.
10
The Maya Today Many Maya people still live near their old cities. Maya people speak more than thirty languages. Their words for house, sun, time, mother, and corn sound alike in all Mayan languages. The Maya still watch the sky, which helps them know when to plant crops.
Picture: Maya people today live near their old cities, speaking Mayan languages and tending fields. A family stands outside a house, watching the sky above the jungle, using what they see to know when to plant their corn crops.
11
Seeing Chichén Itzá Today More than one million people visit Chichén Itzá each year! Visitors used to climb the stepped pyramid if they wanted to. Over time, the steps broke down and became dangerous. The people of Mexico want to keep the building and visitors safe. Today, visitors are not allowed to climb the steps.
Picture: More than a million visitors crowd around the stepped pyramid at Chichén Itzá. The worn, broken steps crumble in places, and visitors stay on the ground below, no longer allowed to climb the dangerous staircase.

A Wonderful Place Chichén Itzá was picked as one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. The city has amazing buildings that show the skill of the Maya people.
Picture: The whole city of Chichén Itzá stretches out in the jungle: the tall stepped pyramid, the Great Ball Court, temples, and carved columns. The amazing stone buildings show the skill of the Maya people who built them.
