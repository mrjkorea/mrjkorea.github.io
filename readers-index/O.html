She worked hard to keep the Everglades as it was to protect the plants and animals that lived there. River of Grass In the 1940s, Marjory Stoneman Douglas wrote a book called The Everglades: River of Grass. The book was a great success. It helped save the special nature in the area.

The Park Badlands National Park (BNP) is located in South Dakota. The park helps protect wild plants and animals. About one million people visit BNP each year. Native American groups have lived there for a long time. They believe the land is special. Land of The Lakota people have lived near Badlands National Park for more than two hundred years. They call it mako sica (mah-KOH SHEE-chah), which means “land that is bad” in their language. Scientists also use the word badlands to describe land where soft rock has been worn away by weather.

Rocks and Grass Geologists are scientists who study rocks, soil, and land to learn more about Earth’s history. BNP makes their job easier because its rocks show how the land formed. Each layer tells a story.
Picture: Geologists study rocks, soil, and land to learn about Earth’s history. In the park, several geologists examine layered rock formations and the ground around them. The visible layers show how the land formed, with each layer telling a story about the past.

The layers contain fossil, or plants and animals that have become rock. The layers show that BNP has gone through big changes. For example, the area was once a jungle, long before humans lived there. It was also underwater for millions of years. Many amazing fossils have been found in Badlands National Park. They include parts of giant rhinoceroses and three-toed horses. In 2010, a seven-year-old Junior Ranger named Kylie Ferguson discovered a fossil of a saber- toothed cat!
