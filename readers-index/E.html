3

Picture: A clear pitcher stands on the kitchen counter beside a bowl of fresh yellow lemons. One child holds the pitcher steady with both hands while the other reaches for a lemon, ready to make lemonade in the pitcher.
4
Add cold water. Add white sugar.

5

The First Strawberry

3
Buy some plants for your strawberry patch.
Picture: At a garden centre, a child lifts a tray of small strawberry plants in little pots. The young green plants have fresh leaves and damp soil around their roots. Shelves of plants and stacked pots fill the background as the child carries the tray.
4
Plant the plants in your strawberry patch. Leave room for them to spread.
Picture: In the sunny garden patch, a child kneels on the soil and presses a small strawberry plant into a hole, patting the earth around it. Several more plants sit in a row, spaced well apart with bare soil between them, room to spread.
5
Spread straw in your strawberry patch. Straw helps prevent weeds and pests.
Picture: The child spreads handfuls of golden straw around the young strawberry plants, tucking it under the green leaves and over the bare soil. The straw covers the ground between the plants in a thick, soft layer, with a fork and a straw bale nearby.
6
Water your strawberry patch at least twice a week.
Picture: The child stands beside the strawberry patch holding a watering can, tipping water gently over the plants and the straw. Droplets soak into the dark soil around each plant, and a second watering can waits on the path behind.
7
The plants in your strawberry patch will bloom. Then, the plants will make small green fruits.
Picture: The strawberry plants in the patch are covered in small white flowers. Some flowers have dropped, and tiny green fruits hang where they were, tucked beneath green leaves. The child bends down close to look at the little green berries forming.

Picture: Low to the ground, the small strawberries on the plants are changing colour. Some are still green, some are pale white-pink, and a few have turned bright red. The child crouches beside the patch, pointing at one red berry among the leaves and straw.
9
Pick those fruits in your strawberry patch. Wash and eat them.
Picture: The child kneels in the patch and picks the ripe red strawberries, placing them into a small basket. Later, at a garden tap, the child rinses the berries under running water and pops one into their mouth.
10
The first strawberry will taste the very best.
Picture: The child sits on the grass beside the strawberry patch, holding one perfect red strawberry up to the sunshine, eyes bright and a wide smile, ready to take the very first bite. The basket of strawberries rests beside them.
